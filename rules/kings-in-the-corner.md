# Kings in the Corner

Kings in the Corner plays like a solitaire layout that everyone shares. You build descending, colour-alternating piles in the middle, and only a king can open one of the four corner spaces. It is calm, quick to teach, and works well across a range of ages. A round takes about 10 minutes.

## What You Need
- **Players:** 2-6. Four is ideal.
- **Deck:** One standard 52-card deck. Aces are low.
- **Extras:** Pen and paper if you are scoring, or counters if you play for chips.

## Key Terms
- **Foundation piles:** The four face-up piles laid out around the stock, at north, east, south, and west.
- **Corners:** The four diagonal spaces (northeast, southeast, southwest, northwest). Only kings may start a pile there.
- **Alternating colours:** Red on black, black on red - hearts and diamonds are red, clubs and spades are black.
- **Stock:** The face-down draw pile in the centre.

## Setup
1. Deal 7 cards to each player.
2. Place the rest face down in the middle as the stock.
3. Turn 4 cards face up from the stock and place one on each side of it - above, below, left, and right. These are your starting foundation piles.
4. Leave the four diagonal corner spaces empty.

## How to Play
Play passes clockwise, starting to the dealer's left. On your turn you may make **as many moves as you like**, then must draw one card to finish.

Legal moves:
1. **Play a card from your hand** onto any foundation pile, if it is one rank lower and the opposite colour to that pile's top card. A red 10 goes onto a black jack.
2. **Start a corner pile** by playing a king from your hand into any empty corner space. Kings can go nowhere else.
3. **Move a whole pile** onto another one, if the moving pile's *bottom* card fits the receiving pile's top card by rank and colour. This is the move new players forget, and it opens up the space the pile came from.
4. **Fill an empty side space** with any card from your hand.
5. **Draw one card** from the stock. This ends your turn.

You may play as many cards as you can arrange in a single turn - a good turn can empty half your hand.

## Scoring & Winning
- The first player to get rid of all their cards wins the round and scores nothing, which is the goal.
- Everyone else counts penalty points for the cards left in their hand: **10 for each king** and **1 for every other card**.
- Play rounds until someone reaches an agreed total, usually 25 or 50 points. The player with the **lowest** score at that point wins.

## Example
The north pile shows a black jack. You play your red 10 onto it, then your black 9 onto that, then move the entire east pile - which starts with a red 8 - on top of your 9. That frees the east space, which you fill with a card from your hand.

## Variations
- **Deal 5 instead of 7** for a faster round with more players.
- **Play for chips:** everyone antes one chip per round, the winner takes the pot, and losers pay a chip per king left in hand.
- **Aces high:** allow aces to be played on 2s as well as under them.

## Tips for New Players
- Look for pile moves before you play from your hand. Moving a whole pile costs you nothing and frees a space.
- Hold a king back if the corners are already full - a king in hand is 10 penalty points, but it is also the only card that can open a corner later.
- Since drawing ends your turn, make every move you possibly can first.
