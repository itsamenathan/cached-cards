# Cribbage

Cribbage is a 400-year-old counting game with its own scoreboard - a wooden track you move pegs along. Every hand you score points twice: once while cards are being played, and once when hands are counted at the end. It rewards spotting number combinations quickly. A game takes about 20 minutes.

## What You Need
- **Players:** 2 is the classic game. 3 and 4 player versions exist.
- **Deck:** One standard 52-card deck. Aces are low and worth 1; face cards are worth 10.
- **Extras:** A cribbage board and pegs if you have one. Pen and paper works fine.

## Key Terms
- **Crib:** A fifth hand made from the cards both players throw away. It belongs to the dealer.
- **Starter (or cut):** A card cut from the deck and used by both hands and the crib.
- **Pegging:** The scoring that happens while cards are being played out.
- **Go:** What you say when you cannot play without passing 31.
- **His heels:** A jack cut as the starter, worth 2 points to the dealer immediately.
- **His nobs:** Holding the jack of the same suit as the starter, worth 1 point at the count.

## Setup
1. Cut for deal - low card deals first. The deal alternates every hand after that.
2. Deal 6 cards to each player.
3. Both players choose 2 cards to put face down into the crib. Think carefully: the crib scores for the dealer, so the non-dealer wants to throw junk into it.
4. The non-dealer cuts the deck and the dealer turns up the top card of the lower half as the **starter**. If it is a jack, the dealer immediately pegs 2.

## How to Play
### Pegging
Players alternate laying cards face up in front of themselves, keeping a running total out loud. The non-dealer goes first.

Score as you go:

| Combination | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Total reaches exactly 15 | 2 |
| Total reaches exactly 31 | 2 |
| Pair (matching the last card played) | 2 |
| Three of a kind | 6 |
| Four of a kind | 12 |
| Run of three or more, in any order | 1 per card |
| Last card played before a "go" | 1 |

If you cannot play without passing 31, say "go" - your opponent keeps playing and scores 1 for laying the last card.

The total can never pass 31. When it stops, reset to zero and continue with the remaining cards.

### The show
Once all cards are played, hands are counted out loud - non-dealer first, then dealer, then the dealer's crib. Add the starter card to each hand so you are always scoring five cards.

Count every combination separately - the same card can score in several of them:

| Combination | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Each distinct set of cards adding to 15 | 2 |
| Each pair (three of a kind counts as three pairs) | 2 |
| Each run of three or more | 1 per card |
| Four-card flush in hand | 4 |
| Five-card flush including the starter | 5 |
| His nobs - the jack matching the starter's suit | 1 |

A flush in the crib only scores if all five cards match suit.

## Scoring & Winning
- The first player to 121 points wins, and you win the moment you get there - you do not finish the hand.
- Because the non-dealer counts their hand first, they can win a close game before the dealer ever counts.
- If your opponent finishes under 91, it is a **skunk** and traditionally counts as a double win.

## Example
You play a 7 for a total of 7. Your opponent plays an 8, making 15, and pegs 2 points. You then play another 8 for a pair, scoring 2 and bringing the total to 23.

## Variations
- **Three-player:** deal 5 cards each plus one straight into the crib.
- **Four-player:** two partnerships, 5 cards each, one to the crib from each player.
- **Muggins:** if you miss points in your own count, your opponent may claim them. Brutal, and traditional.

## Tips for New Players
- 5s are the most valuable cards in the game because so many cards are worth 10. Never throw a 5 into your opponent's crib.
- Leading a 4 is safer than leading a 5, which hands your opponent an easy 15.
- The maximum possible hand is 29 points: three 5s, the J matching a 5-card starter. You will probably never see it.
